                  Keep it balanced

                  An equation is like a set of scales. Whatever you do to one side, do exactly the same to the other, and it stays true.

                  x + 4 = 10subtract 4 from both sidesx = 6

                  Undo with the opposite

                  To get x on its own, undo what's been done to it using the inverse operation.

                  You seeDo this to both sides
                  x + 5Subtract 5
                  x − 5Add 5
                  5xDivide by 5
                  x5Multiply by 5

                  Reading the notation

                  5x means 5 × x. A number written in front of a bracket multiplies everything inside it, so 3(x + 2) means 3 × (x + 2). A fraction line means divide: x4 is x ÷ 4.

                  Order of operations (BIDMAS)

                  Calculations happen in this order: Brackets, Indices, Division and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction. When solving, undo them in reverse: deal with adding and subtracting first, then multiplying and dividing, then brackets.

                  2x + 3 = 11subtract 3 first, then divide by 2x = 4

                  Brackets

                  Either divide both sides by the number outside the bracket, or expand it by multiplying each term inside:

                  a(b + c) = ab + acfor example3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

                  A minus sign in front of a bracket changes every sign inside: −(x + 3) = −x − 3, and −2(x − 5) = −2x + 10.

                  Square brackets are just brackets. They're used when one bracket sits inside another, and you work from the outside in.

                  4[5(x − 3) + 8] = 212divide by 4 first5(x − 3) + 8 = 53

                  Collecting like terms

                  Terms with x combine with each other, and plain numbers combine with each other.

                  4x + 7 + 2x = 6x + 7

                  x on both sides

                  Subtract the smaller x term from both sides, so x only appears once.

                  5x + 2 = 3x + 10subtract 3x from both sides2x + 2 = 10

                  Fractions

                  Multiply both sides by the number on the bottom to clear the fraction. With more than one fraction, multiply by the lowest common denominator: the smallest number they all divide into.

                  x + 32 = 5multiply both sides by 2x + 3 = 10
                  x2 + x3 = 5multiply both sides by 63x + 2x = 30

                  Negative numbers

                  Two minuses multiplied or divided make a plus. Dividing a negative by a negative gives a positive answer.

                  −3x = −12divide both sides by −3x = 4

                  Check your answer

                  Put your value of x back into the original equation. If both sides come out the same, you're right.

                  3(x + 4) = 21 with x = 33 × 7 = 21✓

                  How to play Countdown

                  Use some or all of six numbers to make the target, with + − × and ÷. Like the numbers round on Countdown.

                  The rules

                  Each number can be used once at most, and you don't have to use them all. Every step has to give a whole number of zero or more, so no fractions and nothing below zero.

                  Calculations follow the usual order: × and ÷ before + and −, then left to right. Use brackets to change the order. For example, with 75, 10, 5 and 3, (75 − 5) × 10 + 3 makes 703.

                  Today's puzzle

                  You can see the target straight away. The numbers stay covered until you press Start, then you have 60 seconds. The clock keeps running if you leave the page.

                  Try as many calculations as you like. Your closest result counts, and hitting the target exactly ends the round. If time runs out with no answer, the round counts as out of time.

                  Every puzzle can be solved exactly. When the round ends you'll see one way to do it.

                  Entering a calculation

                  Tap the numbers and symbols, or type on a keyboard. As long as your calculation follows the rules, you'll see what it makes as you go. Press Check to try it. A calculation that breaks a rule tells you why and doesn't count.

                  Past countdowns

                  Puzzles in the archive aren't timed, and they don't affect your streak or statistics. If you're stuck, Show a solution ends the round.

                  Statistics

                  Your streak counts days in a row where you hit the target exactly. The statistics also show how close you got each day: exact, 1–5 away, 6–10 away, 11 or more away, or no answer.

                  How to play Lattice

                  Lattice multiplication (sometimes called Chinese multiplication) lays a long multiplication out as a grid. Each puzzle is a multiplication with some digits hidden. Fill in every ?.

                  The grid

                  One number runs along the top and the other down the right side. Each cell holds its column digit times its row digit, split by the diagonal: tens in the top left, units in the bottom right. Here's 47 × 36:

                  The top-left cell is 4 × 3 = 12, so it shows 1 and 2. The bottom-right cell is 7 × 6 = 42.

                  Adding the diagonals

                  Start at the bottom right and add the digits in each diagonal band. Write the units digit in the box where the band ends, and carry any tens into the next band. The small number in a box's corner is the carry.

                  For 47 × 36: the corner is 2. The next band is 4 + 4 + 1 = 9. Then 2 + 2 + 2 = 6, then 1. Reading down the left and along the bottom gives 1692.

                  Solving a puzzle

                  Everything is connected, so work from what you can see. A few things that help:

                  Look at units digits. Anything times 5 ends in 0 or 5, and an even digit times anything gives an even result. If you know a cell and one of its two digits, the other is usually pinned down.

                  A cell's tens digit tells you roughly how big the product is: 2 × ? in the 10s means ? is 5 or more.

                  Product digits work backwards too. If a diagonal must end in 8 and you know all but one of its digits, you can work out the last one.

                  When two options both fit the grid, try each one. Only one will make every other digit, including the product, work.

                  The rules

                  Neither number starts with 0, and the product never starts with 0. Every puzzle has exactly one solution.

                  How to play

                  Every day there is one new equation. Work out the value of x and enter it. You get 6 attempts, and x is always a whole number.

                  7(x + 4) = 42  gives  x = 2

                  Puzzles get harder through the week: easy on Monday, medium Tuesday and Wednesday, hard Thursday and Friday, expert at the weekend.

                  Stuck? Each hint reveals the next step of the working, and uses one of your attempts. Hints never give away the final answer. The formula book (the book icon) explains the rules for free.

                  When you finish, you'll see the worked solution. Solve on consecutive days to build a streak.

                  A new puzzle goes live at midnight in your time zone. Missed one? Play it from the archive. Archive puzzles don't affect your streak or statistics.

                  Practice gives you unlimited puzzles at any level. They don't count towards your statistics either.

                  Lattice is a second game: a multiplication grid with missing digits. Switch to it at the top. Its guide (the book or ? icon while you're in Lattice) explains how it works.
